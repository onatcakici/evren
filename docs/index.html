How it works

Evren found Pluto in NASA SPHEREx images, on its own.

In 1930 Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto by blinking two photographs of the same stars. Evren runs his method on SPHEREx: it lines up every image of a patch of sky, subtracts what never changes, flags what moved and checks it against every known asteroid and comet.

What moved here

    What Pluto is made of

    SPHEREx does more than take pictures: every pixel sees its own color of infrared light. So each exposure that catches Pluto measures one point of its spectrum. Evren measured Pluto in 72 exposures on 2 of SPHEREx's six detectors, subtracted the same spot on other days to remove the stars behind it, and divided out the color of sunlight.

    The dips are methane ice on Pluto's surface. At 3.3 microns Pluto reflects only about 4 percent of the light it reflects near 1 micron. Astronomers first detected methane on Pluto in 1976. Evren found its fingerprint again in raw SPHEREx images.

    Loading the spectrum

    How it works

    In 1930 Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto by flipping between two photographs of the same stars taken days apart. Stars stay put. Anything that jumps is moving. Evren does the same with SPHEREx, the NASA space telescope that maps the whole sky in 102 infrared colors every six months.

    1. Find every SPHEREx image that covers one patch of sky.
    2. Read only the pixels needed and put every image on one north up grid, using each image's own sky coordinates.
    3. Take the median of all frames as the static sky and subtract it, so only change is left.
    4. Link spots that show up in several exposures of the same visit. A cosmic ray hits once, an asteroid stays.
    5. Check every spot against all known asteroids, comets and dwarf planets. A match proves the method works. No match makes it a candidate worth a second look.

    Reading the views: Frames are the images as SPHEREx took them. Changes shows each frame minus the static sky, so white means something new appeared and black means something disappeared. Static sky is what never changes. Blink flips between two dates, the way Tombaugh did.

    Keys: left and right arrows step, space plays, B blinks.
